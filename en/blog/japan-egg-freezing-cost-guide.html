The Egg Freezing Process: Step-by-Step

The journey to egg freezing in Japan typically spans 2-3 weeks and follows a carefully orchestrated medical protocol:

Step 1: Initial Consultation & Medical Assessment (Days 1-2)

Your process begins with a comprehensive consultation at a Japanese fertility clinic. This appointment includes: - Detailed medical history review - Hormone level testing (FSH, AMH, estradiol) - Ultrasound to assess ovarian reserve and follicle count - Discussion of realistic expectations and outcomes - Financial counseling and treatment plan customization

Many clinics now offer virtual consultations before your arrival, allowing you to complete initial assessments remotely with JMedPass's coordination.

Step 2: Hormone Stimulation (Days 3-12)

Once you arrive in Japan, you'll begin controlled ovarian hyperstimulation. This involves: - Daily injections of FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) to encourage multiple egg development - Regular monitoring through blood tests and vaginal ultrasounds (every 2-3 days) - Medication adjustments based on your response - Detailed instructions for self-administered injections (clinics provide comprehensive training)

Most women tolerate hormone stimulation well, though some experience mild side effects like bloating, slight nausea, or mood fluctuations—all temporary and manageable.

Step 3: Final Maturation & Trigger Shot (Day 12)

Once your follicles reach optimal size (18-20mm), your doctor administers a "trigger shot" (typically hCG—human chorionic gonadotropin). This final injection completes egg maturation and prepares them for retrieval exactly 36 hours later.

Step 4: Egg Retrieval (Day 14)

The egg retrieval is a minor surgical procedure performed under anesthesia. Here's what to expect: - Light sedation or general anesthesia (you won't feel pain or remember the procedure) - A thin needle guided by ultrasound retrieves eggs from each follicle - Procedure typically lasts 15-20 minutes - Recovery takes 1-2 hours; mild cramping and spotting are normal - You can usually resume normal activities within 24 hours

The number of eggs retrieved varies widely (typically 6-20+) based on ovarian reserve and age. Quality matters more than quantity—modern laboratories focus on maximizing healthy, mature eggs.

Step 5: Oocyte Cryopreservation with Vitrification (Day 14-15)

This is where Japan's advanced reproductive technology truly shines. Your retrieved eggs are carefully prepared using vitrification, the rapid-freeze method: - Mature eggs are identified and assessed for quality - Eggs are gradually exposed to cryoprotectants (special substances that prevent ice crystal formation) - Using vitrification technology, eggs are cooled to -196°C in liquid nitrogen in seconds - Eggs enter a glass-like state, perfectly preserved for future use - Survival rates upon thawing are 90-95%

Your eggs are then stored in secure, monitored facilities with redundant backup power systems and continuous nitrogen replenishment.

Step 6: Recovery & Discharge (Days 15-21)

After retrieval, you'll spend several days in Japan for recovery and final consultations. Your fertility team will: - Confirm successful egg preservation - Provide comprehensive care instructions for storage maintenance - Discuss future plans for egg usage (in-vitro fertilization timeline, etc.) - Arrange your medical visa documentation and travel logistics

Frequently Asked Questions About Egg Freezing in Japan

How long can eggs be stored?

There's no established maximum storage duration for properly vitrified eggs. Studies show eggs frozen 10+ years maintain their original quality. Japan permits indefinite storage; we maintain documentation for all stored eggs indefinitely. We recommend periodic check-ins (annual or biennial) to confirm storage status and update contacts.

What are the success rates when thawing frozen eggs?

Modern vitrification achieves 90-95% survival rates upon thawing—meaning 90 of 100 eggs thaw successfully. Fertilization and embryo development rates are comparable to fresh eggs of the same age. Your age when eggs were frozen (not your current age) determines genetic quality and miscarriage rates, which is why early freezing is advantageous.

Can I freeze eggs multiple times?

Absolutely. Many international patients complete 2-3 freezing cycles to build a larger reserve, spacing cycles 2-3 months apart for optimal egg quality recovery between stimulation protocols.

What happens if I never use my frozen eggs?

You have complete autonomy. You can: - Continue storage indefinitely with annual storage fees - Donate eggs to other fertility patients (some programs offer fee waivers with donation) - Discontinue storage and safely dispose of eggs at any time

JMedPass never pressures patients regarding unused eggs—it's entirely your decision.

Can I take hormones at home or do I need to be in Japan?

You must be in Japan for the retrieval procedure itself (non-negotiable for safety and timing). However, you can complete 5-7 days of pre-stimulation hormone injections at home before traveling, shortening your Japan visit to 10-14 days if preferred. Most patients find a full 2-3 week stay optimal for comfort and monitoring.

How much time off work do I need?

Most patients take 2-3 weeks total. Egg retrieval day and the following day require rest. By day 3, most women feel well enough for light activities. Full physical activity (exercise, heavy lifting) typically resumes after one week. You can coordinate your Japan visit during vacation time, and minimal work is required for hormone injection self-administration once trained.

Are there risks or complications?

Egg freezing is generally safe. Possible but rare complications include: - Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS): Mild to moderate in 10% of cases; severe in <1%. Managed with monitoring and hydration. - Infection or bleeding: Extremely rare (<0.1%) with modern retrieval techniques - Allergic reactions to medications: Manageable with pre-screening

Japanese clinics screen extensively for risk factors and adjust protocols accordingly. Vitrification itself carries minimal risk—ice crystal formation (the old concern with slow-freezing) is virtually eliminated.
